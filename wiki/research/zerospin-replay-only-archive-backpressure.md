# Pacing Zerospin replay under archive backpressure

Researched 2026-11-11 against QRK's installed Zerospin 43.1.4 and its matching upstream source. This is a research recommendation. See the [124-command incident][incident] and the [QRK package pins][pins].

## What is worth doing

Keep the AggregateVersionChain (AVC) 64-command backlog limit and its acknowledgment after the local append. First make the AVC wait briefly for actual archive progress on the exact backlog refusal. If capacity is still unavailable, leave the page queued for recovery. For long **background replay**, separately limit the actual pending rows in the AggregateVersionRepo (AVR) outbox before replay adds more. The short wait handles an ordinary burst. The AVR limit is what stops a prolonged archive stall from turning a 100,000-command replay into a 100,000-row AVR queue. Keep an Archive cursor requirement out of the original execution transaction. [AVC append][append] · [AVC backlog check][capacity] · [AVR publication and replay][avr].

The distinction matters because one acknowledgment transfers ownership of the evaluated row from AVR to AVC. A second acknowledgment from the Archive lets AVC prune its local row. Waiting for the second acknowledgment on every successful append would move the first boundary and tie publication to archive latency. The existing handoff test deliberately makes the Archive unavailable and proves that canonical AggregateChain can still hand a command off to AVC's local storage. [Archive fanout][archive] · [Outbox acknowledgment][outbox] · [Existing handoff test][handoff].

## Where replay gets ahead

1. **Cold pull.** AVR activation runs local catch-up inside the constructor gate. `runFanoutCatchup` captures a destination from the first finalized source page, calls the receiver, and rereads durable progress before pulling another page. It uses the same AVR receiver as live delivery. [Activation][activation] · [Catch-up loop][catchup] · [Constructor gate][gate].
2. **Live push.** AggregateChain keeps a separate acknowledged position for each release. A page is acknowledged through its last row only after the entire receiver call succeeds. A failed recipient stays behind for this drain without excluding the others. [Replay registration and acknowledgment][aggregate-chain] · [Delivery acknowledgment][fanout] · [Independent subscriber delivery][fanout-loop].
3. **Canonical versus parallel.** Canonical AVR awaits publication through the page before acknowledging replay, and its catch-up cursor is no further than the first pending output minus one. AggregateChain can then adopt the published history. The parallel AVR reports its materialization head, starts publication in the background, and can replay the next page while the previous results still sit in its outbox. The outbox's 64-row page size limits one read and append, while separate replay pages can keep adding rows. [AVR replay][avr] · [AggregateChain adoption hook][aggregate-chain] · [Outbox page sizing and deletion][outbox].

## Small change at the AVC boundary

1. Keep the transaction's whole-page decision. On `aggregate-archive-backlog-full` only, release the append permit and let the existing archive fanout continue. Wait briefly for the cursor needed for the offered page. For the rejected snapshot this is `localTip + genuinelyNewRows - 64`. Retake the permit and revalidate the page against current history and capacity. The observation grants no reservation. The append's success path still follows AVC's SQLite commit, including an exact retry of a committed page. The append already arms archive recovery, and the Archive can proceed independently of AVR. [AVC backlog check][capacity] · [AVC append permit and wake][append] · [AVC archive delivery][archive] · [Wake versus drain][wake].
2. On a longer stall, end this delivery attempt as an expected deferral. Keep the page and recovery hold. The existing outbox deletes the submitted page only after a successful envelope, so ordinary success cannot encode deferral. The 43.1.4 receiver and generic outbox propagate a typed failure and the invocation logger prints escaping failures. A first pass can classify only the exact expected refusal at the relevant recovery boundary while preserving its non-acknowledgment, or add a distinct deferral outcome if required. An invalid command, failed Archive RPC, or transport failure still needs its own diagnosis. [Delivery loop][delivery] · [Recovery hold][outbox-recovery] · [Invocation logging][logging].
3. This change addresses transient AVC saturation. If the Archive remains stalled, a parallel AVR can still generate later replay output indefinitely unless the producer is paced as well. [Parallel AVR receiver][avr].

## A bound at the replay producer

1. Start with a **one-publication-page threshold of 64 pending outbox rows** for an explicitly identified background replay workload. Count the actual local queue, including restored rows. Inside the existing per-command materialization transaction, identify the next contiguous new local position, then check for a pending row at offset 63 in the indexed outbox before evaluation or restoration. The check and insertion share the execution permit and transaction. The existing path for an already materialized command verifies its retained evaluated command without inserting another row. Restoring a retained result for a position missing from the local view does insert a publication row, even when the matching AVC result was already archived. A difference between the materialization head and the archive cursor would miss those restored rows. [Contiguous-position check][contiguous] · [Replay duplicate lookup and permit][replay-command] · [Transaction branches][materialize] · [Restore writes an outbox row][restore] · [Atomic outbox and checkpoint][retain].
2. On a full queue, leave the next replay command uncommitted. Release the execution permit and leave SQL before draining committed output through a captured local position. Check capacity again before retrying that command. Start with the oldest rows through AVR's normal serialized publication drain. A failed send or lost reply leaves the page in AVR. AVC compares an exact retransmission against its retained evaluated commands before inserting anything. A late append after a local timeout can race with redelivery. The exact-repeat behavior protects that race. [AVR outbox send][avr] · [Outbox transport][transport] · [Whole-page deletion][outbox] · [AVC repeat handling][append].
3. If warm live replay exhausts its wait, do not acknowledge the pushed page. The prefix already committed to the materialization head remains durable. AggregateChain's recovery hold re-drives the page, AVR verifies the existing prefix, and replay continues at the first missing position. The AVR alarm retries AVR publication. AggregateChain's replay alarm retries the source delivery. The current registry schedules held work at approximately one second, subject to the runtime. Expected saturation should leave both responsibilities intact. [Fanout acknowledgment][fanout] · [Fanout failure rearm][fanout-recovery] · [AVR duplicate handling][replay-command] · [Recovery scheduling][recovery].
4. Draining between replay pages is simpler for a single isolated producer. A general pre-page count or drain can race with another pull or push before either commits. The per-command transaction check avoids holding a new replay lock across RPC. If original execution in the same AVR is still allowed to produce without this check, 64 bounds what the gated replay can add, not the total queue under concurrent originals. A real 64-row bound requires a replay-only workload with no such producer, or a separate decision about original execution. The bound counts rows, not bytes. Source and archive read pages stop near 1 MiB after at least one row, and the default AVR sender reads by row count. Neither rule is a bound on stored or evaluated bytes. [Shared materialization][materialize] · [Original execution permit][original] · [Source read page][source-page] · [Archive read page][archive-page] · [AVR sender row limit][outbox].

## Cold startup and the production boundary

1. A paced cold pull can drive its own committed AVR publication directly and observe independent AVC archive progress. AVR's own alarm and incoming events wait behind its constructor gate. Use one deadline for the entire activation attempt, including prior initialization time, within Cloudflare's 30-second gate. If the deadline expires before the captured prefix is complete, activation fails with the committed prefix intact. The next attempt needs to come from AggregateChain's independent held replay drain using a fresh AVR stub, or from a fresh caller. A test should require recovery by AggregateChain after release of the Archive stall without a user request. Treat this as a testable expectation from the code, not a measured result. An existing Actor Repo directly drains pending output before its subscription and retries materialization after specific pressure. That is a useful precedent for the direction of the wait. [Constructor gate][gate] · [Cloudflare gate][cf-gate] · [AggregateChain arms replay recovery][chain-activation] · [Fanout recovery and stub lookup][fanout] · [Actor precedent][actor].
2. **“Replay only” does not by itself preserve original execution availability.** Original finalization waits on replay through its predecessor, and waiting for a direct command result also invokes replay after execution. Both enter the AVR constructor gate when cold. Direct System admission can name a registered noncanonical release, and already admitted originals route by their recorded release. Applying the new wait to all calls to the common replay receiver can therefore create the post-admission pressure path found in Plan 297. Scoping by “noncanonical” alone is insufficient. [Original predecessor catch-up][original] · [Result wait][result] · [Direct admission validation][direct] · [Requested release validation][requested-release] · [Original release routing][routing].
3. Start by proving the replay bound for a candidate workload verified to have no original traffic, including preexisting admitted originals. An explicitly warm background-only policy can leave cold activation and foreground catch-up behavior as they are, but that would not bound cold replay. Applying the bound to candidate startup may delay that candidate's readiness. Extending startup pacing to production AVRs requires an explicit choice between waiting for Archive room behind the readiness gate and changing the readiness/rebuild contract. Changing the foreground wait contract or retrying a new direct submission cannot be hidden inside a replay tuning change. [Current readiness contract][lifecycle] · [Direct execution admits before waiting][direct-wait].

## How to verify scale and recovery

1. Run the 124-result shape and a healthy replay longer than two publication pages with a deliberately delayed Archive acknowledgment. Check that brief pressure resumes inside the short wait, that a real Archive error remains visible, and that the second AVC page cannot be acknowledged before its local commit. [Incident evidence][incident] · [Existing local handoff test][handoff].
2. Hold Archive delivery long enough to fill both queues. Assert AVR stops producing new replay rows at the chosen bound, AggregateChain retains the unacknowledged suffix, and peers can progress. Include a partially committed pushed page, an already archived restoration, a lost AVC reply, and cold reset followed by re-drive. After restoring the Archive, confirm the identical durable positions finish with no duplicate materialization. Verify canonical adoption still waits for AVC publication. [Replay acknowledgment][fanout] · [Canonical replay][avr] · [Existing recovery tests][archive-recovery-test].
3. For a 100,000-command replay, record peak AVR pending rows and bytes, AVC unarchived positions, count and duration of deferrals, and progress across activations. At 64 results per append there are at least 1,563 append pages. A single cold activation would need to average under roughly 19.2 ms per page even before reserving time for initialization. This is arithmetic against the 30-second gate, not an observed throughput limit. Prove progress across attempts in an isolated framework fixture rather than assume a single activation will finish. [64-result batch limit][capacity] · [Cloudflare gate][cf-gate].
