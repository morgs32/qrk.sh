# Zerospin AVC append acknowledgments and archive backpressure

Researched 2026-11-11 against QRK's installed Zerospin 43.1.4. The source links point to the matching release commit. The proposal below is a protocol sketch for Zerospin. [QRK package pins][pins].

## What does the RPC acknowledge?

**The AggregateVersionChain (AVC) saves the results in its own SQLite database before replying with success.** The async method is `appendEvaluatedCommands`. The `receive` in the AggregateVersionRepo (VAR) outbox is a sender-side adapter that awaits that method. AVC commits the new rows and its local history head in one synchronous transaction. It then starts archive delivery in the background and returns a success envelope with a void payload. The reply covers the full submitted page. There is no capacity or archive cursor in it. [VAR adapter][var-adapter] · [AVC append][avc-append] · [SQL transaction][transaction] · [Head update][head-update].

**The Archive writes and acknowledges separately.** AVC awaits `wake()`, which arms recovery and starts the fanout without waiting for the fanout to finish. `waitUntil` can prevent eviction while the background promise settles. RPC completion is independent of that promise. The fanout calls the separate AggregateVersionArchive. After that call succeeds, AVC advances its archive cursor and deletes the covered local rows in the same transaction. An upstream test keeps the Archive unavailable and verifies that the canonical handoff still succeeds with one row committed in AVC and the archive cursor at zero. [Fanout wake][wake] · [Cloudflare Durable Object State][waituntil] · [Archive delivery][archive-delivery] · [Cursor and prune transaction][prune-tx] · [Prune callback][prune] · [Handoff test][handoff-test].

## Why a healthy replay can hit the limit

The VAR commits evaluated results to its outbox with the model changes. Its outbox reads up to 64 rows in order, awaits AVC's reply, deletes the delivered page on success, then immediately reads the next page. AVC independently allows at most 64 new results to be outstanding against its archive cursor. Its capacity check rejects the whole incoming batch before any insert if the existing unarchived count plus the new rows would exceed 64. A valid duplicate consumes no new slots because AVC checks the retained result and filters it out before the new append. [VAR transaction][retain] · [Outbox page size][page-size] · [Outbox read and delete][outbox-storage] · [Outbox drain][outbox-delivery] · [AVC capacity check][capacity] · [AVC append][avc-append].

In the QRK local User V12 trace, the AVC append beginning at 23:55:16.007 UTC inserted #1–64. The append attempts beginning at 16.235 and 16.672 produced no command inserts while the first page still counted against the limit. Once AVC acknowledged the first archive write, the append beginning at 16.936 inserted #65–124. The permanent archive has 124 rows spanning positions 1–124. The evidence is the local Miniflare v3 trace store for `sys_qrk_sh_1`, the V12 Archive database, and the two refusals in local Wrangler log `wrangler-2026-11-11_23-55-07_233.log` at lines 233 and 306. This establishes the ordering and the refusal code. The cause of the archive timing and any connection to the nearby `NOSENTRY` warning remain unknown. [Intended asynchronous handoff and overflow policy][archive-plan].

The timing needs no interruption. If the sender gets the first AVC reply before AVC records the archive acknowledgment, the next page can arrive too early. In 43.1.4 the refusal is a failure envelope. The outbox stops that drain without deleting the page, leaving recovery armed. The overflow policy is intentional. Sending a full page without checking space and surfacing routine saturation as a failed invocation are choices in the current protocol. [AVC capacity check][capacity] · [Outbox drain][outbox-delivery] · [Outbox recovery][outbox-recovery] · [Archive plan][archive-plan].

## What a cursor could tell the sender

AVC already has the two numbers it needs. `acceptedThrough` would be the local history head, and `archivedThrough` would be the highest position whose Archive receipt AVC has applied. From the same snapshot, `available = 64 - (acceptedThrough - archivedThrough)` within the current cap. For contiguous new commands, `archivedThrough + 64` is the highest index that would fit. [AVC capacity check][capacity].

For example, after the first page AVC could reply `{ acceptedThrough: 64, archivedThrough: 0, available: 0 }`. The VAR can remove #1–64 from its outbox and hold #65–124. Once the archive cursor reaches 64, a fresh observation shows 64 free slots and all 60 remaining rows fit. The local acceptance cursor says what the sender may delete. The archive cursor says how far it can send. A snapshot grants no reservation against other appends, so AVC still checks capacity in the append transaction. A zero-credit reply also needs a way to resume, such as an existing recovery wake that rechecks space. [Outbox read and delete][outbox-storage] · [AVC capacity check][capacity] · [Outbox recovery][outbox-recovery].

Three possible changes, in increasing scope:

1. **Preserve full-page acceptance and make deferral an ordinary delivery outcome.** A full reply can carry current space as a hint. When a page cannot fit, report a distinct deferred outcome and leave all its rows queued. Recovery can recheck capacity and retry. This is the smallest option if the goal is to keep the current durability contract and remove the error from normal delivery. A deferred result must skip the success path that deletes the entire page. [Outbox read and delete][outbox-storage] · [Outbox recovery][outbox-recovery].
2. **Wait for space when needed.** AVC can give the existing archive fanout a bounded chance to make progress before refusing the next batch, then recheck capacity in the append transaction. The archive wait belongs outside the SQL transaction and should avoid holding the append permit across the Archive RPC. A slow Archive then adds latency to a saturated append, and a timeout or failure still needs deferred recovery. Awaiting archival on every successful append would also change when the sender gets its receipt. [AVC append][avc-append] · [Fanout wake and drain][wake] · [SQL transaction][transaction].
3. **Accept a prefix and return an explicit acceptance cursor.** If only 12 of 60 fit, AVC could commit those 12 and return the last accepted index. This changes the sender's contract. Today the default acknowledgment deletes the whole page before its optional callback. A prefix receipt would have to delete only the confirmed contiguous prefix, stop cleanly when no rows fit, and preserve lost-reply recovery by validating the retried prefix against retained results. This option is useful if filling partially free space matters. A credit hint with full-page semantics can also size the next page without introducing partial acceptance. [Outbox read and delete][outbox-storage] · [AVC append][avc-append].
